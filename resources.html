Learning Resources

Read, listen, and explore cetacean communication.

A starting point for exploring dolphin communication, animal cognition, and the tools used to study sound.

These links lead to material you can use now. Choose a route based on what you want to understand, then return to the primary sources when you need the evidence behind a claim.

Search these resources by topic →

Build a vocabulary

Start with the glossary when an unfamiliar term interrupts your reading. Use it to distinguish an acoustic description, a behavioral observation, and an interpretation of what an animal might be communicating. A definition is a starting point for asking a more precise question.

Read the glossary →

Find the research

The research archive brings together papers and annotations. Follow the source links to inspect methods, study populations, and limitations. When reading a summary, keep a note of what the underlying study actually measured and how that differs from the headline.

Browse the research archive →

Listen with context

The sound guide introduces whistles, clicks, and burst pulses and points to recordings published by the Wild Dolphin Project. Keep the researchers’ descriptions alongside the audio. The guide explains why a sound alone does not provide a translation.

Explore the sound guide →

Explore a visualization

The visualizer uses synthesized examples to demonstrate how sound appears in different displays. Compare a waveform with a frequency view. These examples illustrate the tool; they are not field recordings or evidence of particular dolphin meanings.

Open the visualizer →

Read beyond a single paper

The reading list gathers books across cetacean communication, cognition, philosophy, and AI. Choose a subject, read its introduction, and use the references to develop a reading path of your own. The links lead to books rather than downloadable copies supplied by this website.

Open the reading list →

Follow the publication

The newsletter brings research questions and editorial context together. Visit its page to learn about the publication, or go to Substack to see the current subscription options. For a question about a source or a suggested correction, contact Noah directly.
